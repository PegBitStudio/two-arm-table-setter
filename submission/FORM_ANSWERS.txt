======================================================================
SUBMISSION TITLE
======================================================================
Two-Arm Table Setter

======================================================================
SHORT DESCRIPTION
======================================================================
Two simulated SO-101 arms set a dinner table from a spoken command: they see with a camera, understand it with Qwen3-VL on OpenVINO, hand items to each other, and fix their own mistakes - on a Core Ultra's CPU, Arc iGPU and NPU.

======================================================================
LONG DESCRIPTION
======================================================================
Say "set the table" - or "put the red thing top left of the plate and the grey utensil on the right" - and two SO-101 arms in MuJoCo do it.

HOW IT WORKS. An overhead colour-and-depth camera finds every object from its height and shape (0.5 mm average error over 20 random tables); the simulator's true positions are never used to act. Qwen3-VL-4B, running locally as Intel's INT4 OpenVINO build, reads the command together with the camera image, so it knows "the red thing" is the mug. A planner decides which arm does what: cutlery starting on the wrong side is passed hand to hand, and round items are set down where both arms can reach. After every item the robot looks again, and anything knocked out of place is put back first.

LEARNING. Our scripted skills became the teacher, recording 1,219 successful demonstrations automatically. A LeRobot ACT policy trained on a laptop CPU learned the mug skill - 19/20 within 1.5 cm on tables it had never seen. It took seven versions; the write-up covers what failed and why.

INTEL OPTIMISATION, on a Core Ultra 7 155H across all three chips. OpenVINO runs the policy 2.9x faster than PyTorch on the CPU (0.72 ms vs 2.07 ms) with no loss of task success. The same policy runs on the AI Boost NPU at 0.90 ms at FP32, and at FP16 there it places 10/10 mugs, freeing the CPU and iGPU. The Arc iGPU reads a command in 1.3 s, 2.8x faster than the CPU. An honest negative result too: OpenVINO 2026.3's NPU compiler cannot build Qwen3-VL's vision tower, so the language model falls back to the iGPU automatically.

RESULTS. 30/30 random tables set correctly, 29/30 on harder ones, 9/10 on the Core Ultra. Every number is checked against simulator truth, reproducible from one command, and the raw logs ship in the repo. Not included, said plainly: the drawer and pouring, and most skills are scripted rather than learned.
